# Manual away-from-ball turn experiment (2026-10-01)

Milestone `ms-22.1` ? plan `plan-22.1.2` ? task `task-22.1.2.2`. This branch tests whether a deliberate manual turn away from a distant loose ball should override an automatic BallControl/Trap request. The override requires in-play manual control, no player possession, ball distance above 0.7 m, and input opposed both to current motion and to the predicted ball direction. Explicit passes and shots keep priority. The earlier momentum recovery and post-touch movement requeue are retained; the pre-touch animation-offset experiment is omitted.

The real standalone-game/private-D3D12/XTEST diagnostic ran 15 independent windows, seeds 42?56, four presses per window. It admitted 60/60 inputs, observed 59/60 qualifying velocity responses, and measured velocity p95 114.821 ms with 8 presses above the 50 ms budget or unanswered. It therefore **fails product acceptance**. The previously versioned formal baseline measured 51/60 responses, p95 218.951 ms, and 22 violations. The two runs are not paired; their difference is diagnostic rather than proof of a causal improvement. The closest seed-56 follow-up observed 4/4 responses with p95 62.330 ms, also above budget.

The reusable `feel_touch_override_review.py` finds 6 presses with an actual controlled-player touch in this candidate and 12 in the baseline. Gameplay trajectories diverged, so this count alone cannot establish whether any required touch was lost. The remaining long-tail examples include seed 48/press 2: a pending Trap touch toward the ball delayed movement acceptance until 303.497 ms; and seed 44/press 3: a movement command was accepted at 19.626 ms but the physical response arrived at 286.434 ms. The former still needs touch-preserving control; the latter needs faster movement trajectory response.

`engine_ai_touch_contract` passed 586 assertions on the candidate engine. `engine_native_bot_selection_contract` stopped at `Semantic authority diverged`: its fixed gameplay hash fixture does not describe the changed candidate. Deterministic replay and full formal acceptance have not been revalidated, so this implementation remains isolated from `master`.
